# What a good job description contains

**Job descriptions, not personas.** The document exists so a dispatcher, a reviewer, and a
stranger six months from now can all answer the same three questions: what is this seat
accountable for, when does work reach it, and what will it refuse.

Personality is not in scope. "Meticulous, detail-oriented, passionate about quality" tells
a router nothing and tells a reviewer less.

## The sections

Most orgs enforce a subset of these mechanically. Declare yours in `jobDoc.requiredSections`
and `hire` refuses to write a doc that misses one.

| Section | Answers | Fails when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Desk** | is this seat active, folded, or paused? | folded seats keep taking routed work |
| **Runtime** | what model and effort does it run at? | cost is invisible until the bill |
| **Job** | what does it *own* — the outcome, not the activity | the seat becomes a job title |
| **Inputs → outputs** | what arrives, what leaves, where it lands | nobody can point at its work |
| **Routed when** | the conditions that send work here | everything routes to the generalist |
| **Quality bar** | what good looks like, checkably, and who checks | unreviewed output ships |
| **Boundaries** | limits, and the hard NEVERs | it does the thing you assumed it wouldn't |
| **Escalation** | who it goes to, and what it does with nobody awake | it invents a rule at 3am |
| **Earns the seat when** | the metric that justifies the hire | the roster only grows |
| **Folded when** | the trigger that ends the seat | folding becomes a public argument |

## Writing rules

1. **Ownership sentences, not activity lists.** "Every partner relationship reaching a yes
   or a no within two weeks" beats "handles partner communications".
2. **NEVERs are absolute or they are not NEVERs.** If there is a case where it is fine,
   that case belongs in Boundaries, and the NEVER belongs somewhere else.
3. **Name the reviewer.** A person, "self-review only", or the owner. Not blank.
4. **The decommission condition is written by the person hiring.** Anyone can write it
   later; nobody does.
5. **One line per section.** A job description that needs three paragraphs to say what a
   seat owns is describing more than one seat.
